# Academic

Academic answers two questions you normally cannot answer without a calculator
and a copy of the syllabus:

**What is my grade right now?** and **can I still get the grade I want?**

It does that by holding the whole term, not just the parts that have been marked.

Last updated 2026-10-10.

---

## How it fits together

```
   Semester            Fall 2026, Spring 2027, one at a time
      |
      +-- Course       CS 201, MATH 241
             |
             +-- Category      Exams 40%, Homework 30%, Quizzes 30%
                    |
                    +-- Item   Midterm, HW 3, Quiz 5
```

A semester holds courses, a course says how it is graded, and every piece of
graded work is an item inside one of those buckets.

---

## Setting up a course

**Start with a semester.** Courses live inside one, so each term keeps its own
subjects and last term stays where you left it. The pencil beside the picker
reopens its name and start date, for when one of them came out wrong.

**Then add the course and say how it is graded.** Give each bucket a name and a
weight. The names are yours: if your syllabus says Assignments rather than
Homework, type Assignments.

The buckets have to add up to 100. The form keeps a running total and will not
let you save until it does, because a course that adds up to 90 or 110 quietly
gives you the wrong grade all term.

**Drop lowest.** If a course throws away your two worst quizzes, set the quiz
bucket to drop 2. It only ever drops among the quizzes that already have a
score, so early in the term it drops less, and it changes its mind as new
scores come in. That is deliberate: it always shows the best case available to
you today.

**Extra credit.** Tick the extra box on a bucket and it sits on top of the 100
rather than inside it. A course can then total 105, and a perfect term reads as
105%, not as 100% of 105.

**Letter cutoffs.** A is 90, B is 80, C is 70, D is 60 by default. Change them
per course if yours grades differently.

## Filling in the term

Type in every assignment at the start, straight from the syllabus, and leave the
score blank. This is the one habit the whole module is built around.

An item with no score is not a zero. It is work that has not happened yet, and
the difference is what lets Academic tell you three separate things instead of
one confusing number.

When a mark comes back, type it into the score box and press Enter, or just
click somewhere else. It is saved once, when you leave the box, not after every
key, so a fast typist can never end up with half a number stored.

Rows stay in the order you put them in; you can drag them, see **Putting things
in the order you want** below. The list sorted by date is **Coming up**, beside
the table. Typed something wrong? The pencil on the row reopens the name, the
points it is out of and the date. The score you have already entered is kept.

The bucket headings have a pencil too, for when the name or the weight came out
wrong. That saves opening the whole course just to fix one number.

## The three numbers

```
  Current grade        84%  B
  Earned               42 / 100   C
  Maximum attainable   92%  A
```

**Current grade** is how you are doing in what has actually been marked. It does
not sink just because the final has not happened. This is the number your
school would show you.

**Earned** is how much of the final 100 you have already locked in. It starts at
0 in week one and climbs all term. Once it passes a grade boundary it carries
that letter, because that is the grade you would walk away with even if you
never handed in another thing.

**Maximum attainable** is where the course finishes if you take full marks on
everything that has not been marked yet. It starts at 100, or higher when there
is extra credit, and comes down only when you actually lose points. It is the
honest answer to "is the grade I want still on the table", and it carries its
letter so you can see that at a glance. The letter is left off once the ceiling
has fallen below a pass.

They answer different questions and each is worth having. A current
grade of 95% feels great until you notice you have only earned 12 points.

### A worked example

Exams are worth 60, homework 40. The midterm came back at 90, the final has not
happened, and all the homework is in at 80.

Half the exam weight is marked, so 30 of the 60 points count so far. Homework is
finished, so all 40 count.

```
  marked so far   30 + 40 = 70 points
  earned          30 x 90%  +  40 x 80%  =  27 + 32  =  59
  current grade   59 / 70   =  84%
  earned          59
  still ahead     30
```

## Coming up

Beside the grade table is a list of everything still waiting on a score in
**every course of the semester**, soonest first, so nothing is missed by not
opening the right course. Each row shows the date, the item, then one line
under it: the course code with its colour dot, the bucket, and how far away it
is.

```
  Sep 24   Quiz 2
           MATH 221 · Quizzes · 2 days late
  Oct 2    Essay draft
           ENGL 102 · Essays · in 6 days
```

On a phone the list sits above the grade table, on a tablet it runs in two
columns, on a computer it stays beside the table.

Anything already marked drops off the list, so what is left is exactly what you
still owe. **Anything due within three days, or already late, is written in
red**, so the things that need doing this week stand out from the ones that can
wait.

Items with no due date do not appear here. They still count toward your grade,
they simply have nothing to be listed by.

## Putting things in the order you want

Courses, the grading buckets, and the rows inside each bucket all have a grip
handle on the right. Hold it and drag: what you are holding follows the cursor
and everything else slides out of the way, so you can see where it will land
before you let go. The order is saved the moment you release.

Courses drag sideways along the strip. Buckets and rows drag up and down.

**The rows inside a bucket follow the order you set, not their due dates.** That
is on purpose: this list is your syllabus in the order you think about it, while
Coming up is the list sorted by date. Trying to sort one list by both would mean
one rule quietly overriding the other.

## Working with the calendar

**Academic and your calendar are kept separately for now, on purpose.** Putting
an exam on your calendar does not create a row here, and adding a row here does
not put anything on your calendar. Each one is typed where it belongs.

So an exam usually gets written down twice: once in Schedule so you know when to
turn up, and once in Academic so it counts toward your grade. That is a little
more typing in exchange for never wondering which side is telling the truth.

Exams on the calendar do not repeat. A weekly quiz is a row per quiz in
Academic; your calendar only needs to say when you sit them.

## Things worth knowing

**Deleting cascades.** Removing a course removes its buckets and every score in
them. Removing a semester removes its courses. There is no undo here yet, unlike
the calendar, so deleting a semester asks you to confirm first.

**Which course you have open is remembered on this device**, so a reload lands
where you left off. Your phone and your laptop can sit on different courses at
the same time without fighting.

**The semester opens itself.** Academic shows the most recent term that has
already started. Pick another one whenever you want to look ahead or back.

**Your data lives in your account**, not in the browser. Sign in anywhere and it
is all there.

**A tab that has been sitting in the background refreshes itself when you come
back to it.** If you have LifeOS open twice, the one you are not looking at will
not update live, but it catches up the moment you click into it.

## How it looks

The Academic page is **blue**. Its colours live in
`app/academic/academic.css`, one block for light mode and one for night:

```
light  canvas #d3e4fc   surface #e0edff   surface-muted #caddf8
       line #b4cef4     line-strong #8fb6ed   accent-soft #bfd9fe
       ink #151d28      ink-muted #394a61     ink-faint #44546a
       accent #0059bd
```

At night it uses the app's base night colours. Nothing on the page is pure
white in light mode: like every page, the surfaces are a toned version of the
page colour. The tab icon is `app/academic/icon.svg`, a white L on `#0059bd`.

**Your course colour shows on every course card** in the strip, as a dot
beside the code and as the progress bar, and fills the whole card of the course
you have open. It also colours the current grade figure and the dot beside the
course name. The dot uses the deeper tone of the colour, because the pale one
disappeared against the blue page.

**Each number shows once.** Under the course name sits one row: Current grade,
large, then Earned out of 100 and Maximum attainable, each with its letter.
Under that, **the grade track**: one bar from 0 to 100 with what you have
earned solid, what is still possible striped, and a tick for each letter cutoff
from the course's scale. The three big boxes and the track's own legend were
removed on 2026-11-01 because they repeated the same figures.
`components/grade-track.tsx`.

**New course is a round plus button** at the end of the course strip; it used
to be a dashed card the size of a course, which wrapped onto a row of its own on
a tablet. **Delete semester lives inside the semester's edit form**, opened by
the pencil beside its name, in red, and still asks before it deletes anything.

**A row's pencil, bin and grip show when the pointer is over the row**, or when
the keyboard reaches one of them. On a touch screen, which has no hover, they
are always there.

**Letter grades always have the same colours**: A green, B blue, C yellow,
D pink, F red, the same pastels the calendar uses for events.

**Anything due within three days, or late, is red**, the same red that marks the
current time on the calendar and money going out in Finance.

## Under the hood

```
app/academic/page.tsx                  the route, imports the view and academic.css
modules/academic/components/
  academic-view.tsx                    the page
  semester-bar.tsx                     the semester picker and its pencil
  course-strip.tsx                     the row of courses along the top
  course-form.tsx                      adding and editing a course and its buckets
  grade-table.tsx                      the buckets and their rows
  upcoming.tsx                         Coming up
  letter-pill.tsx                      the coloured letter
  confirm.tsx                          the delete confirmation
modules/academic/lib/
  grade.ts                             every number on the page
  course-store.ts                      reading and writing, the only file that knows the database
  selection.ts                         which semester and course are open on this device
```

Four tables hold it: `semesters`, `courses`, `categories` and `grade_items`,
each owned through `public.users` the same way calendar events are. Their
columns and the reasons behind them are in `../CLAUDE.md` section 8f.

233 unit tests in `modules/academic/tests` and 25 end to end in
`tests/e2e/academic`, out of 606 and 78 for the whole app. `structure.md` shows
how Academic sits beside the other pages.
